# `stats` — the diagnostic, and why it measures nothing

**Every angle the eval holds is a price.** It says so itself, beside its own verdicts. A harness
change that made runs do less work scores clean on all of them whether or not the work was any good,
and nothing in the set reads what a run came back with. The obvious answer — a model-scored quality
angle — was ruled out on the record, and the conditions it would have to meet before it stops being
a number somebody made up are in the angles topic rather than here.

The same ruling found one model use that holds today, and it is a different product from a verdict:
asking a reviewer what went wrong in a named set of runs, citing the evidence and separating what it
saw from what it concluded, produces findings somebody can act on without any score at all. That is
this reading. It is judged by the findings it corroborates and the issues it earns, and by nothing
else.

## Why it is not an angle

An angle asks one question: did this figure move, further than this corpus's own adjacent windows
move anyway. That question has a fixed shape — a scalar per window and a floor under it — and this
reading has none of it. No scalar, no floor, no before-and-after, no disposition. Putting it in the
set would not extend what an angle means; it would empty the word.

So it is a subject of its own rather than a flag on the eval, and the two never meet: the eval's
source names this one nowhere, and a case holds that. A reading that could reach the eval's `--json`
would end up in the record a release writes, and from there in a chart.

## Why the caller names the runs

A window is the eval's unit because a comparison needs two of them. A diagnostic compares nothing, so
a window buys it nothing, and inheriting one would put a boundary into the reading that no line of it
is about. The set is named three ways — a count, a window of time, or the issues whose runs to read —
and one reading takes one of them, because every line printed is about that set and two ways of
naming it is two readings sharing a screen.

What it could not reach is printed with the reason. A key that no run of the corpus ever owned is the
common case, and a conclusion drawn over a set the reader cannot reconstruct is worth nothing
whatever it says.

## Why a finding that cites nothing is not reported

The reviewer is handed labels: `R1`…`Rn` for the runs and `R1/12` for a call inside one, and it is
told to cite them. A finding whose citation resolves to no call of the set is left out, and the
number left out is printed. Two reasons, and the second is the one that matters. The first is that a
reader has to be able to reach the evidence without trusting the summary. The second is that a
diagnostic is the easiest thing in this CLI to fill with plausible prose, and a citation that has to
resolve against a set the reader also holds is the cheapest check there is on that.

**What a resolved citation proves, and what it does not.** It proves the evidence is reachable: the
reader can go to that call and look. It proves nothing about whether the prose above it follows from
what is there, and no amount of code here could — that judgement is the reader's, and a reading that
implied otherwise would be the verdict this one exists not to be.

The cost is real and is stated rather than hidden: a true finding written loosely is dropped by the
same rule. That is why the count of what was dropped prints beside the count of what was kept — a
reading that quietly threw findings away would read exactly like a clean corpus.

## The four outcomes, and why three would not do

A reply can carry findings, carry findings that cite nothing, carry no finding at all, or carry
nothing this reader recognises. The last two are not the same answer and a reading that collapsed
them would report a corpus with nothing wrong in it every time the reviewer wandered off the
contract. So the contract closes on a line naming the count, the way this CLI's own review contract
does, and a reply without that line as its last is reported as unread, with the reason.

**The count is reconciled and not merely looked for.** A footer naming a finding over a reply whose
openers this reader did not recognise would otherwise come back as a completed reading with nothing
in it — which is the shape of a clean corpus, arrived at by the parser failing. So the number on the
closing line is counted against the blocks above it, and a reply where the two disagree is unread
too.

## What the reviewer is actually shown

A digest and never a transcript whole: each run's calls in order, numbered, each with its class, an
excerpt of what it ran and an excerpt of what came back. Twenty runs of raw transcript are orders
past any payload the gateway takes, and the character budget is shared out between the runs — the
separators taken off the top, since a bound that counts the blocks and not what joins them is a bound
the message walks past — so a set of one is shown far more of that run than a set of twenty is of
each. A set so large that a run's heading alone will not fit its share is refused by name rather than
sent short, because a heading with no call under it is a run the reviewer was told about and shown
nothing of. Each run says how many of
its calls it holds against how many were shown, and the screen says the calls are excerpted.

**And it is masked before it is cut.** A transcript is every line a run typed and every line that
came back, which is a wider surface for a credential than any source file a consult sends, and this
one leaves the machine. It goes through the same mask the refusal log uses, and before the cut rather
than after — a bound that split a secret would leave the half the mask no longer recognises.

That mask is a floor and not a guarantee: it holds the shapes and the names it knows, and a
credential in neither travels. Reading it for this purpose found a hole worth naming, since it is the
shape of the next one: the mask held two lists of credential names that disagreed, so `--codex-key`
— this CLI's own documented flag — was written down whole where `CODEX_KEY=` was not. One list now,
and a pair of cases beside it. What a payload leaving the machine buys the log is that somebody reads
the mask against a real corpus rather than against the shapes it was written for.

**An excerpt is taken from both ends.** A refusal names the rule it refused under on its last line
and a gate prints its verdict there, so a body cut from the front loses exactly the half this reading
exists to find. What a bound cut is unknown to the reviewer, and it is told so in those words rather
than left to assume the call was quiet.

## Why the model call is logged under a kind of its own

`forge codex stats`, `eval` and `replay` all count over answered consults. A diagnostic written into
that log as a consult would move every figure the evaluator reads, and the reading exists to help
that evaluator. So the row goes in under its own kind, which those readers do not admit, and the
consult corpus is what it was before the reading was taken.

## What is deliberately not here

Any score, rating or trend over this reading's output, and any route by which it runs on its own. It
is asked for, by a person or by the role that reads the eval, and what it produces is read once.
