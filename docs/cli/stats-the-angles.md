# `stats` — the angles, and the floor a verdict is against

**Five questions were asked of this harness in one afternoon and none of them was asked of the verb.**
Each was answered by a session that read the same corpus again by hand off `--json`, and two of the
five produced a wrong number first — both times by pairing a numerator over one population with a
denominator over another. Nothing about that was an accident of care. A reading that renders one
fixed table per subject makes every question the chosen rows do not answer into a derivation somebody
does outside the reader, and a population that leaves the reader stops being anybody's.

So one corpus read serves several angles, the reading names the population each is over, and the
reading says what a difference means rather than only what it is.

## Why the set is closed

An open shape — the verb takes a named angle and computes it — grows without a release and nothing
can hold it: no case can be written for an angle nobody has shipped. Closed makes every new question
a change to this repository, which is the cost, and it makes every angle checkable, which is why
`stats.mjs`'s subject map has always been closed too. `--angles` selects from the shipped set in the
spelling `forge codex consult --angles` already uses; it does not extend it.

## Why an angle names its population, and why they are not all the window's runs

Four populations are in the shipped set, and only the first is the window's runs. `guide-reread` is
over the run-and-part pairs those runs read, which is a different count, moves differently, and is
the exact shape of the mistake this topic exists to prevent — a rate of the form "N re-reads over M
reads" mislabels its numerator, because the aggregate counts run-part pairs read more than once
rather than extra reads. The prices are over the runs the API billed, which is the window's runs less
any whose records carry no measurement, and `cache-read-per-request` is over the requests inside
those runs. Both numbers print on both sides of every block. Two angles over different populations
are two readings, and a reader who cannot see which is which has been handed the same trap in a
nicer format.

A price is judged as anything else here is, and the judgement says nothing about whether the harness
got better: a run that did less spent less. What a price is, what it is counted over and what it
does not say is its own topic.

## Why the verdict is against this corpus's own history and not against zero

A difference between two windows means nothing until you know how much two windows differ anyway. On
this project, two adjacent windows of fifty runs differ by **14% in median wall at the median and by
more than half at the 95th percentile** — measured over every adjacent position the corpus admits,
with the reading's own profile on both sides. A verdict against zero would call almost every window
an improvement or a decline.

**What the floor is not.** It is not a null over an unchanged harness. Nine releases landed on this
project in a single day and the corpus's history holds every one of them, so its positions cross real
changes. It is the historical adjacent-shift reference: how far two adjacent blocks of this corpus
have in fact differed, taken everywhere the comparison could have been taken. Real changes being
inside it makes it wider than a true null and therefore a conservative gate — which is precisely what
the third disposition says, and why that disposition is worded about this harness compared with
itself rather than about an unchanged one.

**The floor's own population is its own.** A geometric position is not a reading: a historical block
can hold no run that read a guide part, or a figure of zero, and a position whose before figure is
zero yields no relative shift at all. Those are counted as yielding none, and the quantiles, the
twenty-position minimum and the exceedance share printed beside the verdict are every one of them over
the positions that yielded one. The rule this topic states for the reading is applied to the reading's
own floor first.

Twenty is where a p95 stops being the largest shift the floor happened to see. Under it there is no
verdict, and the count is printed rather than a number that reads like a finding.

## Why a held reading's side is recomputed rather than read

`--against` and `--since-release` put a stored reading in the before window's place. A profile stored
under an older admission or extraction rule carries **the same field names** as one computed now and
measures a different population — two admission tests over one of this repository's own transcript
sets disagreed by a third. A missing-field check passes and the verdict reports the reader's change as
the harness's.

So no angle reads a figure off a stored profile. The before side is recomputed here, from the live
corpus, over the span the held window covers, and the block prints how many runs that span still
recovers against how many the reading recorded. Both sides then come off one reader. Where the span
recovers too few runs to judge, the answer is the fourth disposition and not a delta with a caveat
beside it.

**A mark stores a span and never the runs that were in it**, so the two counts on the block are what
that span recovered and what the reading recorded, and equal counts are not proof of the same
membership: a run wholly inside the span that the held window did not hold is inside it still. What
the span recovers is therefore reported rather than assumed — a corpus that has been swept since gives a smaller number, and the
reader sees it. The bound has to be one every member of a window satisfies, which is why the profile's
own `from` is the earliest start across its runs rather than the first run's: the eval hands it a
window ordered by each run's *end*, and read as the start of the span the first run's own start
excluded a run that began before it and ended inside it.

## Why the angles are not in a mark

`readingOf` is the one assembly both mark writers spread into `writeMark`. An angle folded there would
be stored in every record a ship writes and would spend its floor during a release. The angles are
computed beside the reading, in the screen's own call, and a stored reading holds exactly the keys it
held before they existed.

## What is deliberately not here

The seconds a call spends by class. It is one angle, it is separately filed, and this topic is the
shape it is an instance of rather than a second copy of it.
