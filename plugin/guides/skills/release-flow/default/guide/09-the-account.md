## The account

Written at the end of every run, whichever way it went, in five parts:

1. **The situation.** What this release was, where it was going, and where it stood when you arrived.
   A reader who knows none of it starts here and is not sent anywhere else to begin.
2. **The evidence observed.** The readings themselves — what answered, as what, when — with the part
   of any log you chose quoted directly and the reason you chose that part. Your summary of a reading
   is not the reading.
3. **The diagnosis, graded.** Confirmed cause, leading hypothesis, unresolved uncertainty; every one
   of the three that is true, and the grade attached to each claim rather than to the section.
4. **The actions, each with the alternative it beat.** Not a list of what you ran — for each action,
   what else would have closed the same gap and why this one was chosen over it. That is the part a
   later reader needs and the part a log cannot supply.
5. **What a person should do next.** An act, an owner, and what happens if it is not taken. *Monitor*
   is not an act; *nothing, this is finished* is, and is worth writing where it is true.

The account is the run's output and not a footnote to it. A machine already decided pass or fail
before you were asked; what only you can leave behind is why, and a run that writes five thin
sections has spent its whole budget producing something nobody can act on.
